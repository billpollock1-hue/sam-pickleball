The Problem

Games Have Gotten Less Competitive

SAM's player pool has grown a lot more diverse in skill since 2022. Today's match gap is roughly 1.4x as uneven as it was in early 2022 — closely matched games (a rating gap under 200 points) have fallen from 69% of all games to 53%.

The Core Flaws

Two Structural Problems

Step is a court-movement counter, not a skill score — and it's blind to turnout. Reaching Court 1 on a two-court day means beating half the field. On a five-court day, it means beating four-fifths of it. Step treats both accomplishments as identical.

COURT-COUNT BLIND — a step earned on a slow day and a step earned on a big day carry the same weight, even though one was a meaningfully harder result than the other.

Worse: the shuffle SAM runs today between Shootout 1 and Shootout 2 — the Den's "2-up/2-back" option — doesn't fix this. On today's system, Shootout 1 courts average a 522-point internal skill spread; after 2-up/2-back moves the top two and bottom two on every court, that spread actually widens to 617. The rule meant to correct mismatches is mostly producing counterproductive movement instead.

The Fix

Two Phases, Already Built and Tested

PHASE 1 — Used ratings to seed S1, gentler shuffle Seed Shootout 1 by rating instead of step and percentage, then use the Den's own gentler shuffle setting for Shootout 2. Already tested live, start to finish.
+22% vs today · combined spread 445
PHASE 2 — Results-driven Shootout 2 Shootout 2 courts rebuilt from Shootout 1 results, weighted by opponent strength — movement is earned, not mechanical. Same technique, extended to Shootout 2.
+34% vs today · combined spread 377

Today's baseline: 570 combined spread, 68% of players moving mid-morning.
